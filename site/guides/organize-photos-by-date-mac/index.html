Organize photos into date folders on Mac — automatically, without touching the originals

Updated August 2026

DCIM/103APPLE, New Folder, from old laptop, Untitled Export — camera imports and phone dumps don't come organized, and hand-sorting thousands of files by date is nobody's weekend plan. The fix is a dated folder structure built automatically from each photo's own timestamp.

Why date folders beat every other scheme

Folders like 2024/06/15 have outlived every photo app, cloud service, and operating system since digital cameras existed. They sort chronologically in Finder, they're searchable, every backup tool understands them, and they'll still make sense in thirty years. Organizing by event or topic sounds nice but requires decisions for every file; dates are already in the files.

Where the date actually comes from

This is the part most tools get lazily wrong. A photo's date can live in several places, in decreasing order of trustworthiness:

  • EXIF capture date — written by the camera at the moment of capture. The gold standard, present in most photos and videos.
  • Filename patterns — IMG_20240615_142301.jpg, WhatsApp's IMG-20240615-WA0001.jpg, screenshot names. Reliable when present.
  • File-system dates — creation and modification time. Least trustworthy: copying a file often rewrites them, which is how a 2009 photo ends up "created" in 2021.

A good organizer resolves dates in that order and — critically — tells you which source it used and how confident it is, instead of silently filing a decade of photos under the day you copied them off the old drive.

The safe workflow

  • Copy, never move. Build the organized library as verified copies in a new destination. The source (SD card, phone dump, old drive) stays read-only until you trust the result.
  • Preview before copying. See every file's resolved date and the exact folder it will land in, before anything happens.
  • Review the uncertain ones. Files with missing or conflicting dates should be flagged for you to confirm or fix — not guessed silently.
  • Verify the copies. Byte-for-byte, before the run is called done.
